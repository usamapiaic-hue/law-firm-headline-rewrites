Law Firm Headline Rewrites

20 phrases that commonly raise attorney advertising concerns, and safer versions that still get the call. By Legal Web Agency.

Most bar complaints about law firm websites start with a single line of copy: a superlative, a promise, or a title the lawyer is not allowed to use. The rewrites below keep the persuasion and drop the risk. Rules differ by state, so check yours before publishing; our attorney advertising rules by state hub links all of them. This is general information, not legal advice.

1. Superlatives and comparisons

RiskySaferWhy
"The best car accident lawyer in Houston""Houston car accident lawyers who answer the phone, day or night""Best" is a comparison most firms cannot prove. Concrete service claims can be verified.
"#1 divorce firm in Orange County""Orange County divorce lawyers focused only on family law"Rankings need a named, verifiable source and often a disclaimer.
"Top-rated injury attorneys""Read what our clients say" (linked to real reviews)Let the reviews carry the claim. See reviews on a law firm website.
"Unmatched courtroom skill""We prepare every case as if it will go to trial"Describes the process, not an unprovable comparison.

2. Outcome promises

RiskySaferWhy
"We win.""We fight for the result your case deserves."Implies a guaranteed outcome.
"Get the maximum compensation you deserve""Find out what your claim may be worth in a free consultation"Avoids promising a result.
"$10 million recovered last year"Same figure, plus "Prior results do not guarantee a similar outcome."Many states require the disclaimer next to results. See the case results page guide.
"No recovery, no fee, guaranteed""You pay no attorney's fee unless we recover money for you. Case costs are explained in your agreement."Contingency claims must say who pays costs.

3. Titles and specialization

RiskySaferWhy
"Expert criminal defense attorney""Criminal defense attorney with 15 years in [County] courts""Expert" and "specialist" are restricted in many states unless certified.
"Estate planning specialist""Our practice is limited to estate planning and probate"Describes focus without claiming a certification.
"Board certified" (when not)Remove it, or name the certifying body exactly when true.Certification claims usually need the certifying organization named.

State examples: Texas, Florida, New York, California, New Jersey, Illinois, Georgia and Pennsylvania.

4. Testimonials and endorsements

RiskySaferWhy
A paid actor's testimonial with no labelReal client words, or a clear "dramatization" labelUndisclosed dramatizations can mislead.
"Mark got $250,000 for his back injury!"The quote, plus a results disclaimer and the client's written consent on fileResults in testimonials carry the same risks as case results.
Reviews copied from Google with editsUnedited excerpts with a link to the sourceEdited reviews can become misleading.

5. Urgency and solicitation

RiskySaferWhy
"Act now before it's too late!""Deadlines apply to injury claims. Call to find out yours."Accurate urgency, without pressure tactics.
"Free consultation, guaranteed same-day settlement offer""Free consultation. We will explain your options on the first call."Do not promise what depends on the other side.
Pop-up chat that claims to be a lawyer when it is notLabel it: "Intake team" or "Virtual assistant"Misrepresenting who is answering can mislead.
